Songs of the Church

Privacy Policy

Effective 3 August 2026 · Last updated 4 August 2026

The free app has no account, no sign-in, and no analytics. All 715 songs ship inside it and it works with no network at all. Nothing you do in the app is profiled, sold, or used for advertising — there is no advertising, and there are no tracking SDKs of any kind.

Songs of the Church Plus, the optional yearly subscription, does need an account — one purchase has to work on every device you own, and that is not possible without something to tie it to. What that involves is set out under Subscriptions and accounts below.

This policy covers the Songs of the Church apps for iOS, Android, Windows, macOS and Linux, and the web version at natehale05-gif.github.io/Songs.

What stays on your device

The app saves a few small preferences locally, using the operating system's standard app storage (browser local storage on the web). None of it is transmitted anywhere.

StoredWhy
Which songs you marked as favouritesTo show your Favorites list
How many times you opened each songTo build the Popular list from your own use, rather than from a server
The app version you dismissed an update prompt forSo the same prompt isn't shown again

That is the whole list. Everything else — the set list you are building, your reading font size, whether the app is light or dark — is either held only while the app is open or read from your device's own system setting.

Deleting the app removes all of it. On the web, clearing the site's data in your browser does the same.

Camera

On phones, the app can open the camera for one purpose: reading a small-group leader's join QR code. Frames are decoded on the device and are never recorded, saved, or sent anywhere. Nothing but the join code is read out of the image. The camera is only opened after you tap Scan, and the app works fine if you decline the permission — you can type the join code instead.

Small groups

A small-group session mirrors the leader's current song and verse onto the members' screens. There are two ways to run one, and they behave differently.

Same WiFi

The leader's device hosts the session directly on the local network and answers join requests broadcast over it. No internet connection is used and no data reaches us or any third party — the traffic never leaves the network you are already on. On iOS this is why the app asks for local network access. Being device-to-device on a private network, that connection is not encrypted: there is no certificate to check when the other end is simply another phone. Anyone already on the same network could in principle see which verse a group is on, which is the same thing they could see by looking at the screen.

Online

Because phones on cellular sit behind carrier NAT, a group spread across networks cannot connect device-to-device. In Online mode both the leader and the members connect out to a relay server, which matches them by join code and forwards the leader's current verse. While a session is running, the relay handles:

  • the join code
  • the display names the leader and members typed in
  • the current song title and the text of the verse being shown

The relay keeps only the latest state in memory, so a late joiner lands on the right verse, and forgets a session once it goes idle. It has no database and never writes session content to disk. Its operational log records only that a device joined a session and how many are running — not the join code, the names, or the verse. Beyond that it sees the connection metadata any web server sees, such as your IP address.

Traffic to the relay is encrypted in transit (wss://) but it is not end-to-end encrypted: whoever operates the relay can see which verse a group is on, and anyone who learns a live join code can follow along. Join codes are random out of roughly 887 million combinations, so guessing one is impractical — but please treat an online session as "not secret". Same WiFi mode avoids the relay entirely.

Subscriptions and accounts

None of this applies unless you subscribe. The free app never asks you to sign in, and everything above stays true of it.

The account

To subscribe you create an account with an email address. That is the only personal detail asked for, and it exists for two reasons: to sign you in on a second device so the subscription follows you, and to reach you about the subscription itself — a receipt, a renewal, a price change, or a failed payment. It is not used for marketing, and it is not shared or sold.

What is stored against it

  • Your email address.
  • Whether the subscription is active, and when the current period ends.
  • Which store the purchase came from, and the store's own identifier for it, so a renewal or cancellation can be matched to your account.

That is the whole record. Your favourites, your set lists and which songs you open are not part of it — they stay on your device exactly as described above, and they are not uploaded when you subscribe.

Payment details

We never see your card. Purchases on iPhone, iPad and Android are handled entirely by Apple and Google, who tell us only that a subscription for your account is valid and until when. Purchases on the web and desktop go through Stripe, who process the payment and hold the card details; we receive a confirmation and the last four digits on the receipt, nothing more.

How long it is kept

While your account exists, and for as long afterwards as tax and accounting rules require records of a sale to be kept — typically several years, and not something we can delete on request. You can close your account at any time by asking; doing so ends the subscription and removes the account record apart from what those rules require us to retain.

Checking your subscription

The app asks the subscription server, from time to time, whether your account is still entitled. That request carries your account's sign-in token and nothing about what you have been singing or reading. If it cannot reach the server the app keeps working — it holds on to the last answer for a while rather than locking you out, which matters because this app is built to be used with no connection.

Checking for updates

On launch the app asks GitHub which version is newest, so it can offer you the update. That request goes to api.github.com and, like any web request, tells GitHub your IP address and the app version you are running. It sends no identifier of you or of anything you have done in the app. If the request fails the app carries on silently — it is built to work offline, so it never treats "no network" as a problem. GitHub's handling of that request is covered by the GitHub Privacy Statement.

The web version is hosted on GitHub Pages, and downloads are served from GitHub Releases, so GitHub receives the ordinary server request information in those cases too.

What the app does not do

  • No account or sign-in anywhere in the free app, and never a phone number. Subscribers give an email address and nothing else.
  • No advertising, and no advertising or attribution SDKs.
  • No analytics, crash reporting, or usage telemetry of any kind.
  • No tracking, no device or advertising identifiers, and no data shared with data brokers. Nothing is used to track you across other apps or websites.
  • No location, contacts, photo library, microphone, or health data.
  • No selling or sharing of personal information, as those terms are used in the CCPA/CPRA.

Children

The app is suitable for all ages and does not knowingly collect personal information from children under 13. The free app asks for nothing at all and has no public profile. Subscriptions are not intended for children — they are bought by adults, and an account requires an email address; if you believe a child has created one, contact us and it will be removed. The only text a child could enter is a display name for a small-group session, which is shown to the rest of that group and is discarded when the session ends.

Your rights

If you have never subscribed, nothing is collected about you and there is no stored profile to access, correct, export, or delete. Everything described at the top lives on your own device and is under your control: delete the app, or clear the site data in your browser, and it is gone. If a live session is running, closing it clears the relay's copy of the current verse.

If you have subscribed, you can ask for a copy of the account record described above, ask for it to be corrected, or ask for the account to be closed and deleted — subject only to the sales records that tax rules oblige us to keep. Use the contact address below; there is no charge, and you will not be asked why.
